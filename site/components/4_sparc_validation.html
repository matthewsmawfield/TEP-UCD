3. The SPARC Galaxy Analysis: Phantom Mass as Unscreened Time-Field

Galaxies can be understood as "baryon-anchored" solitons—where the same scalar field structure calibrated from terrestrial GNSS data is pinned to a baryonic mass concentration. This section extends the test to galactic scales, where the $M^{1/3}$ scaling is expected to govern the transition between screened (Newtonian) and unscreened (phantom mass) regimes. The SPARC database provides an ideal testbed: 175 disk galaxies with high-quality rotation curves and well-constrained baryonic mass distributions. The theoretical basis for the phantom mass interpretation is developed in detail in Smawfield (2025e), which demonstrates how differential proper-time accumulation in gravitational lensing can produce apparent dark matter phenomenology.

Phantom Mass Interpretation

In the TEP framework, the apparent "dark matter" observed in galaxy rotation curves is identified as Phantom Mass—defined as the apparent mass derived from temporal shear under the assumption of isochrony (Smawfield 2025e). It is not a particle species but a geometric manifestation of the unscreened proper-time field. A critical distinction must be emphasized: "Phantom Mass" is an observational inference artifact (analogous to a refractive index effect), not a "ghost field" in the quantum field theory sense. Ghost fields imply negative kinetic energy and Hamiltonian instability; phantom mass carries no such pathology. It arises simply from modeling a bi-metric spacetime with a single-metric prior—the "missing mass" is the unmodeled temporal shear contribution.

The transition radius $R_{\rm trans}$ marks the boundary between two regimes:

  • Inside $R_{\rm trans}$: The enclosed mean baryonic density exceeds the critical screening density ($\bar\rho > \rho_{\rm trans}$). The time-field is screened, and gravity follows Newtonian predictions based on visible baryonic matter.
  • Outside $R_{\rm trans}$: The enclosed mean density drops below $\rho_{\rm trans}$. The time-field becomes unscreened, and the refractive proper-time gradient produces an apparent gravitational excess—the "phantom mass" conventionally attributed to dark matter.

If this interpretation is correct, the radius at which rotation curves diverge from Newtonian prediction should scale as $R_{\rm DM} \propto M_{\rm bar}^{1/3}$. While the fundamental core saturation occurs at the proximity scale observationally proxied by $\rho_T \approx 20$ g/cm³, the halo transition is governed by a much lower proximity scale, proxied by $\rho_{\rm trans}$, characteristic of the diffuse field tail.

Invariant: $\rho_T$ denotes the universal saturation density of the scalar sector that fixes the compact soliton scale via $R_T \propto (M/\rho_T)^{1/3}$. By contrast, $\rho_{\rm trans}$ is an emergent screening threshold for the onset of halo phenomenology in diffuse environments; it is not treated as a second fundamental constant and can depend on coupling, geometry, and baryonic structure.

Theoretical Expectation from \(\rho_T\)

The TEP scaling law \(R_T \propto M^{1/3}\), anchored by the GNSS calibration \(\rho_T \approx 20\) g/cm³, has a direct consequence for galactic structure. In the TEP framework, the dark-matter onset radius \(R_{\rm DM}\) is identified with the radius at which the enclosed mean baryonic density drops below the emergent halo threshold \(\rho_{\rm trans}\). Because \(\rho_{\rm trans}\) emerges directly from the two-branch Gauss–Klein–Gordon flux equation \(\nabla\cdot(P_{,X}\nabla u) = (\beta_A/M_{\rm Pl}^2)\rho_b\) inside rising 3-D baryonic cores (where the coupled-gradient crossing acceleration \(g_{\rm crit}(\tau=1.30) = 1.031 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m s}^{-2}\) [$4.49 \times 10^{-11}\text{ m s}^{-2}$ uncoupled] and kinetic column-density scale \(\Sigma_* = 582\,M_\odot\text{ pc}^{-2}\) set the characteristic 3-D core density \(\rho_{\rm core} \sim \Sigma_*/(2h_z) \approx 0.5\,M_\odot\text{ pc}^{-3}\) and enclosed transition density \(\bar\rho_{\rm trans} = 3g_{\rm crit}/(4\pi G R_{\rm DM}) = 0.068\,M_\odot\text{ pc}^{-3}\) at \(R_{\rm DM} = 2.6\text{ kpc}\); see the Formal Link box below and Step 14), dimensional analysis in the 3-D rising-core regime (\(M_{\rm bar} \lesssim M_{\rm cross}^{\rm SPARC}\)) yields:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:dm_scaling} R_{\rm DM} \propto \left(\frac{M_{\rm bar}}{\rho_{\rm trans}}\right)^{1/3} \propto M_{\rm bar}^{1/3}. \end{equation}

Thus the TEP framework expects \(\alpha = 1/3\) for the DM onset scaling. This is a theoretical consequence of the same saturation scale that fixes the terrestrial correlation length, not a new free parameter. The SPARC analysis below tests whether the empirical exponent \(\alpha_{\rm SPARC}\) is consistent with this expectation. Notably, in the unscreened low-density outer halo (\(\rho \ll \rho_T\)), the coupled-gradient quadratic \(a_\phi(a_\phi + g_{\rm bar}) = g_{\rm bar}a_{\rm eff}\) with \(g_{\rm obs} = g_{\rm bar} + a_\phi\) algebraically recovers the MOND simple interpolation function \(g_{\rm bar} = g_{\rm obs}^2/(g_{\rm obs} + a_{\rm eff})\) [\(\mu(x) = x/(1+x)\), \(x = g_{\rm obs}/a_{\rm eff}\)], explaining why TEP reproduces the tight \(0.1330\text{ dex}\) radial-acceleration scatter of MOND (\(0.1328\text{ dex}\)) from the two-branch kinetic operator \(P_{,X}(\xi) = k\sqrt{\xi} + 2\xi\) without an ad hoc interpolation ansatz, while predicting both the 3-D rising-baryon core regime \(R_{\rm DM} \propto M_{\rm bar}^{1/3}\) (\(\alpha = 0.256 \pm 0.069\), consistent with \(1/3\) at \(1.1\sigma\) and \(3.5\sigma\) below the point-mass acceleration-tail exponent \(1/2\)) and five non-galactic \(\rho_T\)-saturated channels where acceleration-only MOND makes zero predictions: (i) terrestrial GNSS atomic-clock spatial coherence at \(L_c \approx R_T(M_\oplus) \approx 4{,}200\text{ km}\) fixed by \(\rho_T \approx 20\text{ g/cm}^3\); (ii) outer-planet equatorial rotation \(v_{\rm eq} = GM/(R_T v_{\rm gal})\); (iii) the Tannock et al. brown-dwarf period floor \(P_{\min}^{\rm th} = (k_{\rm MOI}/\eta)\tau_T(\rho_T) = 1.155\text{ hr}\); (iv) the Solar Model S convection-zone base \(\sqrt{3}R_T(M_\odot) = 0.715\,R_\odot\); and (v) relativistic lensing (Paper 4) and bi-metric gravitational-wave propagation \(\Xi(z) = (1+z)^{-1}\) (Paper 22).

The analysis was performed post hoc: the SPARC data were examined after the TEP framework was established, so the comparison is a consistency check rather than a blind prediction. A measured exponent \(\alpha_{\rm SPARC} \neq 1/3\) (outside measurement uncertainty) would challenge the framework, while agreement supports it.

Methodology

The analysis proceeds as follows for each of the 175 SPARC galaxies:

  1. Calculate the total baryonic mass: $M_{\rm bar} = M_* + 1.33 M_{\rm HI}$, where $M_* = (M/L)_{3.6\mu} \times L_{3.6}$ with $(M/L)_{3.6\mu} = 0.5\,M_\odot/L_\odot$ for disk populations.
  2. Compute the expected Newtonian rotation velocity using the SPARC signed-contribution convention: $V_{\rm bar}^2 = |V_{\rm gas}|V_{\rm gas} + (M/L)_{\rm disk} |V_{\rm disk}|V_{\rm disk} + (M/L)_{\rm bulge} |V_{\rm bulge}|V_{\rm bulge}$, preserving negative $V_{\rm gas}^2$ in central H I depressions.
  3. Identify the mass discrepancy onset radius $R_{\rm DM}$: the first radius where $V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm bar} > 1.3$. Radial bins with nonpositive or unreliable baryonic velocity ($V_{\rm bar} \leq 5$ km s$^{-1}$, including $V_{\rm bar}^2 \leq 0$ from negative model contributions) are excluded from the ratio; galaxies whose ratio never exceeds the threshold contribute no $R_{\rm DM}$ and are dropped from the fit — a selection, disclosed here — leaving 168 of 175 systems at the fiducial threshold (or 167 under unsigned $V_{\rm gas}^2$).
  4. Fit the power-law relation $R_{\rm DM} = k \cdot M_{\rm bar}^\alpha$ across the full sample.

Results

Of the 175 SPARC galaxies, 168 yield valid mass discrepancy onset radii spanning five decades in baryonic mass ($10^7$–$10^{12}\,M_\odot$).

Robustness to Onset Definition

To address concerns that the "onset radius" is a noise-sensitive functional, a bootstrap analysis (1000 resamples) was performed across multiple definition criteria. Size-Scaling Confound Test

The $M^{1/3}$ onset exponent is not by itself discriminating: for approximately self-similar exponential disks, a fixed velocity-ratio crossing occurs at a nearly fixed multiple of the disk scale length $R_d$, so the galaxy mass–size relation can produce the same exponent without any density threshold. The confound is tested directly on the same SPARC sample (pipeline step 8). The in-sample mass–size relation is $R_d \propto M_{\rm bar}^{0.322 \pm 0.017}$ ($r = 0.821$, $n = 175$), which alone predicts an onset exponent $\approx 0.32$ — within the stated uncertainty of the measured $0.352 \pm 0.043$. Comparing the two candidate invariants per galaxy across all discrete first-bin crossings ($n = 168$) — the size ratio $R_{\rm DM}/R_d$ and the enclosed mean baryonic density $\bar\rho(<R_{\rm DM}) = 3M_{\rm bar}(<R_{\rm DM})/(4\pi R_{\rm DM}^3)$, with $M_{\rm bar}(<R)$ estimated spherically as $V_{\rm bar}(R)^2 R/G$ — the size invariant is mass-independent on the raw discrete grid at the fiducial threshold (slope $0.053 \pm 0.044$, $p = 0.23$; median $R_{\rm DM}/R_d = 0.65$), while the enclosed density retains a residual mass correlation (slope $0.196 \pm 0.069$, $p = 4.7\times10^{-3}$). However, a diagnostic of the discrete grid reveals the cause: in $93$ of the $168$ galaxies ($55.4\%$; $112/169$ at threshold $1.2$ and $80/162$ at $1.4$), low-mass dwarfs already satisfy $V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm bar} > 1.3$ at their innermost sampled rotation-curve point ($\text{idx} = 0$), so the discrete estimator is left-censored at the innermost beam/sampling radius $R_{\rm min}$ rather than measuring the true interior threshold crossing. Because rotation-curve radial grids scale with optical/H I disk size ($r(\log_{10}R_{\min},\,\log_{10}R_d) = +0.410, p = 4.6\times 10^{-5}$ across the $93$ censored systems, with median $R_{\min}/R_d = 0.396$ at mean $\log_{10}[M_{\rm bar}/M_\odot] = 9.51$ and negative internal slope $\mathrm{d}\log_{10}(R_{\min}/R_d)/\mathrm{d}\log_{10}M_{\rm bar} = -0.245, p < 10^{-4}$), pooling these $93$ telescope-sampling-floor points with the $75$ resolved interior crossings (mean $\log_{10}[M_{\rm bar}/M_\odot] = 10.17$, median $R_{\rm DM}/R_d = 1.236$, internal slope $+0.116 \pm 0.055$) produces a classic Simpson's-paradox cancellation on the raw $N = 168$ grid (apparent pooled slope $+0.053 \pm 0.044, p = 0.23$) while artificially inflating the apparent $M_{\rm bar}^{1/3}$ residual scatter from $0.366$ dex ($n = 75$ resolved crossings; $0.359$ dex OLS and $0.180$ dex against enclosed mass $M_{\rm bar}(<R_{\rm DM})$) to $0.483$ dex. Evaluating a left-censored Tobit maximum-likelihood regression across all $N = 168$ galaxies (retaining all $93$ left-censored low-mass systems as upper bounds $R_{\rm DM} \le R_{\min}$ alongside the $75$ resolved interior crossings, Step 8) rejects pure disk-scale invariance ($R_{\rm DM}/R_d = \text{const}$) with Tobit slope $+0.323 \pm 0.034$ ($z = 9.57, p < 10^{-4}$; whereas a right-censored Tobit regression on enclosed density $\rho_{\rm enc}(R_{\rm DM}) \ge \rho_{\rm enc}(R_{\min})$ gives a mass-invariant slope of $-0.112 \pm 0.131, p = 0.39$ at threshold $1.2$ and $-0.155 \pm 0.014$ at $1.3$, and because low-surface-brightness dwarfs with $\Sigma_0 < \Sigma_{\rm crit}$ have $R_{\rm DM} = 0$ physically, treating them as log-normal upper bounds $\log_{10} R_{\rm DM} \le \log_{10} R_{\min}$ pulls the low-mass tail toward $-\infty$ and inflates the apparent Tobit radius slope to $0.61 \pm 0.05$ above the resolved interior crossing slope $0.417 \pm 0.050$, which lies between the $M^{1/3}$ saturation scaling and the Step 12 two-branch kinetic $M^{1/2}$ acceleration-crossing slope $0.502$). Likewise, on the $n = 75$ resolved interior crossings at the fiducial $1.3$ threshold, the enclosed baryonic density is the sole statistically mass-invariant candidate among the three physical onset hypotheses (slope $0.075 \pm 0.075$, $p = 0.32$, Spearman $p = 0.56$; scatter contracting from $0.82$ to $0.55$ dex), whereas the disk-size ratio $R_{\rm DM}/R_d$ exhibits a residual mass trend (slope $0.116 \pm 0.055$, $p = 0.039$, Spearman $p = 0.018$; at threshold $1.4$, $n = 82$, both $R_{\rm DM}/R_d$ at $+0.162 \pm 0.048, p = 1.2\times10^{-3}$ and $\rho_{\rm enc}$ at $-0.147 \pm 0.074, p = 0.049$ show residual mass trends) and the local baryonic acceleration at onset $g_{\rm bar}(R_{\rm DM}) = V_{\rm bar}^2(R_{\rm DM})/R_{\rm DM}$ is strongly mass-dependent (slope $+0.492 \pm 0.045$, $p = 3.8\times10^{-17}$). To test kinematic vs. photometric scaling without sharing $R_{\rm DM}$ on both axes (as would occur if $R_{\rm DM}$ were regressed directly on $M_{\rm bar}(<R_{\rm DM}) = V_{\rm bar}^2 R_{\rm DM}/G$), Step 8 evaluates the non-circular partial correlation of $\log_{10} R_{\rm DM}$ with the purely kinematic velocity $\log_{10} V_{\rm bar}(R_{\rm DM})$ controlling for $\log_{10} R_d$ across the $n = 75$ resolved crossings, yielding $r_{\rm part}(R_{\rm DM},\,V_{\rm bar}\mid R_d) = +0.754$ ($p = 5.6\times10^{-15}$) versus $r_{\rm part}(R_{\rm DM},\,R_d\mid V_{\rm bar}) = +0.171$ ($p = 0.14$, statistically null; or $r_{\rm part}(R_{\rm DM},\,V_{\rm bar}(2.2R_d)\mid R_d) = +0.547, p = 3.8\times10^{-7}$ vs. $r_{\rm part}(R_{\rm DM},\,R_d\mid V_{\rm bar}(2.2R_d)) = +0.224, p = 0.053$ when $V_{\rm bar}$ is evaluated at the fixed photometric disk peak $2.2R_d$ with zero dependence on $R_{\rm DM}$). Furthermore, to test whether the intermediate full-sample slope ($0.417 \pm 0.050$) genuinely reflects a physical transition between the 3-D volume-density core regime ($\alpha = 1/3$) and the outer 2-D Keplerian acceleration tail ($\alpha = 1/2$) rather than post-hoc range flexibility, the $n = 75$ resolved galaxies are partitioned a priori — using only their Newtonian baryonic rotation curve $V_{\rm bar}(r)$ with zero dependence on $V_{\rm obs}$ or $R_{\rm DM}$ — via the Freeman (1970) exponential-disk rotation profile (Step 8). Combining the in-sample SPARC mass–size relation ($R_d \propto M_{\rm bar}^{0.322}$) with the Freeman peak acceleration $g_{\rm peak}(M_{\rm bar}) = 0.180\,GM_{\rm bar}/R_d^2$ predicts a regime crossover mass of $M_{\rm cross}^{\rm SPARC} = 1.45 \times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$ ($\log_{10}[M_{\rm cross}/M_\odot] = 10.16$), below which disk peaks fall below the crossing acceleration and transitions occur inside the rising 3-D volume-density core ($\alpha_{\rm pred} = 1/3 = 0.333$), and above which transitions occur outside the turnover in the 2-D Keplerian tail ($\alpha_{\rm pred} = 1/2 = 0.500$). Partitioning the $n = 75$ resolved galaxies a priori at the exact analytical Freeman logarithmic velocity slope between $1.0\,R_d$ and $2.2\,R_d$, $\gamma_{\rm Freeman} \equiv \ln[V_{\rm Freeman}(2.2R_d)/V_{\rm Freeman}(1.0R_d)]/\ln 2.2 = 0.210$ (zero dependence on $V_{\rm obs}$ or $R_{\rm DM}$), galaxies with rising inner baryonic curves ($\gamma_V \ge 0.210$, $n = 38$, median $M_{\rm bar} = 5.9 \times 10^9\,M_\odot < M_{\rm cross}^{\rm SPARC}$, pre-specified $\alpha_{\rm pred} = 1/3$) yield $\alpha_{\rm rising} = 0.256 \pm 0.069$ (consistent with $1/3$ at $1.1\sigma$, rejecting $1/2$ at $3.5\sigma$), whereas galaxies with concentrated or flat baryonic curves ($\gamma_V < 0.210$, $n = 37$, median $M_{\rm bar} = 4.8 \times 10^{10}\,M_\odot > M_{\rm cross}^{\rm SPARC}$, pre-specified $\alpha_{\rm pred} = 1/2$) yield $\alpha_{\rm tail} = 0.581 \pm 0.086$ (consistent with $1/2$ at $0.95\sigma$, rejecting $1/3$ at $2.9\sigma$; subsample shift $\Delta\alpha = +0.325 \pm 0.110$, $z = 2.95, p = 0.0031$). Decomposing the selection sensitivity between the full discrete sample ($N = 168$, $\alpha = 0.369 \pm 0.041$) and the resolved subset ($n = 75$, $\alpha = 0.417 \pm 0.050$) shows that $+0.028$ of the $+0.048$ difference arises from excluding the $93$ $R_{\min}$-censored low-surface-brightness dwarfs (on the discrete grid, the $n = 75$ uncensored subset gives $\alpha = 0.397 \pm 0.045$), while $+0.020$ arises from sub-bin log-linear interpolation.

Phantom-Mass Amplitude and Profile Test

The onset radius alone does not test the mechanism — any threshold criterion produces an onset. The discriminating content of the phantom-mass claim lies in the amplitude and the radial profile, both of which are evaluated directly on the same SPARC sample (pipeline step 9). In a canonical linear-kinetic scalar baseline ($P_{,X}\to 1$) with conformal charge $\beta_A$, an unscreened scalar channel contributes a centripetal term of order $2\beta_A^2$ times the Newtonian one, giving the linear-kinetic plateau ceiling $V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm bar} = \sqrt{1 + 2\beta_A^2 \mathcal S_{\rm eff}}$, which for the bare coupling $|\beta_A| = 1$ at $\mathcal S_{\rm eff} = 1$ is $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.73$. Measured on the outer third of each curve ($n = 164$ galaxies with $\geq 6$ valid points and $V_{\rm bar} > 5$ km s$^{-1}$), the median observed-to-baryonic plateau ratio is $1.98$ ($16$–$84$th percentile $1.38$–$2.49$) — exceeding the linear-kinetic bare ceiling ($\mathcal S_{\rm eff} = 1.46$ at the median, with $71\%$ of galaxies requiring $\mathcal S_{\rm eff} > 1$). Crucially, evaluating the selected two-branch nonlinear kinetic completion $P_{,X}(\xi)=\epsilon_{\rm reg}+k\sqrt\xi+2\xi$ ($k=16.03$, zero free parameters) directly on the same 164 outer-third galaxy plateaus closes this linear-kinetic gap completely: the median observed-to-predicted plateau velocity ratio under the coupled-gradient additive law $a_\phi = \sqrt{(g_N/2)^2 + g_N a_{\rm eff}} - g_N/2$ is $V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm pred}^{\rm TEP} = 0.952$ ($16$–$84$th percentile $0.814$–$1.110$, RMS $0.094$ dex) at the canonical $\Upsilon_d = 0.50$ and $1.009$ ($16$–$84$th percentile $0.870$–$1.158$, RMS $0.091$ dex) at $\Upsilon_d = 0.35$ (with $0.893$ and $0.952$ under uncoupled self-gradient addition, and $1.011$, RMS $0.090$ dex under the quadrature comparator).

The radial profile supplies the second discriminator. Defining the enhancement $E(R) = V_{\rm obs}^2/V_{\rm bar}^2 - 1 = M_{\rm ph}(R)/M_{\rm bar}(<R)$, the phantom fraction is required by the transition picture to grow outward (screened inside, unscreened outside), whereas a constant coupling rescaling of the baryonic potential predicts $d\log E/d\log R = 0$. The measured profile rises: median slope $0.46$ across 161 galaxies with sufficient baseline, positive in 133 (Wilcoxon signed-rank $p = 2.3\times10^{-17}$). The rising phantom fraction is the expected morphology of a baryon-anchored screening transition and is inconsistent with a global multiplicative rescaling; it is not, by itself, separable from acceleration-threshold phenomenologies, which predict the same qualitative rise. What the two tests jointly establish is that the SPARC sample carries both an amplitude-level and a profile-level constraint on the mechanism — the onset scaling is no longer the sole empirical content of the claim.

Cross-Regime Consistency

The significance of this result emerges when combined with the Temporal Topology screening analysis (Section 4). Across macroscopic compact and planetary bodies (Section 4), the saturation radius $R_T(M) = (3M/4\pi\rho_T)^{1/3}$ defines the transition into the screened compact-body regime ($S \ge 1$), whereas in diffuse galactic disks ($S \ll 1$) the onset of the phantom-mass regime transitions from $M^{1/3}$ core scaling in rising-baryon disks ($M_{\rm bar} \lesssim M_{\rm cross}^{\rm SPARC} = 1.45 \times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$) to $M^{1/2}$ low-acceleration scaling in concentrated disks ($M_{\rm bar} \gtrsim M_{\rm cross}^{\rm SPARC}$).

Combined with the RBH-1 Schwarzschild crossover ($R_T/R_S = 1.32$) and the Milky Way inner mass-discrepancy onset ($R_{\text{DM}} \approx 3.1$ kpc, observed $\sim 3$–$5$ kpc), these results link the compact-object saturation sector and the low-acceleration galactic kinetic sector within the two-branch TEP framework.

Physical Interpretation

The observed $M^{1/3}$ scaling admits a natural interpretation in terms of density-limited screening. If the screening transition occurs at a characteristic density $\rho_{\rm trans}$, dimensional analysis requires:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:trans_radius} R_{\rm trans} \sim \left( \frac{M}{\rho_{\rm trans}} \right)^{1/3}. \end{equation}

Fixing $\alpha_{\rm SPARC} = 1/3$ and fitting only the normalization yields $k \approx 8.0 \times 10^{-4}\,{\rm kpc}/M_\odot^{1/3}$ ($7.96 \times 10^{-4}$). This normalization corresponds to an emergent screening density $\rho_{\rm trans} \approx 0.5\,M_\odot/{\rm pc}^3$ ($\sim 3 \times 10^{-23}$ g/cm³) recovered from the fitted normalization $k$ via Eq. ($\ref{eq:trans_radius}$). As derived in the Formal Link box below (Eq. [\ref{eq:profile_link}], Step 14), this transition density follows directly from the two-branch kinetic operator $P_{,X}(\xi) = \epsilon_{\rm reg} + k\sqrt{\xi} + 2\xi$ ($g_{\rm crit} = 1.031 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m s}^{-2}$ coupled-gradient [$4.49 \times 10^{-11}\text{ m s}^{-2}$ uncoupled], $\Sigma_* = 582\,M_\odot\text{ pc}^{-2}$) with zero free parameters. This value is within an order of magnitude of typical disk densities at the optical radius (0.01–0.1 $M_\odot/{\rm pc}^3$), providing a physically reasonable anchor for the screening mechanism.

Note: This galactic transition density $\rho_{\rm trans}$ is distinct from the fundamental core saturation scale $\rho_T \approx 20$ g/cm³ calibrated in Section 2 and cross-checked in Section 5. The core density $\rho_T$ governs the compact object size (soliton surface), while $\rho_{\rm trans}$ governs the onset of the diffuse halo effect (screening radius). Both scales follow the same $M^{1/3}$ structural form, consistent with the environment-dependent nature of the theory across 15 orders of magnitude in density.

In the two-branch TEP action (Paper 0 §7; Appendix C.2, Eq. $\ref{eq:nonlinear_chameleon_eom}$; Step 14), compact-object saturation is governed by the potential sector $V(u) = \tfrac{1}{4}\lambda M_{\rm Pl}^4 u^4$ at $\rho_T \approx 20\text{ g/cm}^3$, whereas diffuse galactic halo onset ($\bar\rho \ll \rho_T$, where $V_{\rm eff}'(u) \to 0$) is governed directly by the nonlinear kinetic operator $P_{,X}(\xi) = \epsilon_{\rm reg} + k\sqrt{\xi} + 2\xi$ ($\xi \equiv |X|/\Lambda_X^4$, $\Lambda_X = \sqrt{M_{\rm Pl}H_0} = 1.91\text{ meV}$, $g_t = cH_0/2 = 3.40 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m s}^{-2}$, $a_{\rm eff} = 4\sqrt{2}\,g_t/k = 1.20 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m s}^{-2}$):

\begin{equation} \label{eq:profile_link} \nabla\cdot\bigl(P_{,X}(\xi)\nabla u\bigr) = \frac{\beta_A}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}\,\rho_b \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \bar u\,P_{,X}(\bar u^2/2) = \frac{g_N(r)}{g_t}. \end{equation}

At the fiducial velocity-ratio crossing $\tau = V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm bar} = 1.30$ (where $a_\phi/g_N = \tau^2 - 1 = 0.69$ and $g_{\rm obs}/g_N = \tau^2 = 1.69$), solving the coupled-gradient force relation $a_\phi(a_\phi + g_N) = g_N\,a_{\rm eff}$ fixes the Newtonian crossing acceleration $g_{\rm crit}(\tau=1.30) = a_{\rm eff}/[\tau^2(\tau^2 - 1)] = 1.031 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m s}^{-2}$ ($3.18 \times 10^3\text{ [km s}^{-1}]^2\text{ kpc}^{-1}$; or $4.49 \times 10^{-11}\text{ m s}^{-2}$ under the uncoupled self-gradient comparator $a_\phi = \sqrt{g_N a_{\rm eff}}$) and the kinetic column-density scale $\Sigma_* = \Lambda_X^2/(\sqrt{2}|\beta_A|M_{\rm Pl}) = 0.122\text{ g cm}^{-2} = 582\,M_\odot\text{ pc}^{-2}$ (Step 14). Converting $g_{\rm crit}$ into an enclosed mean baryonic density via $\bar\rho_{\rm trans}(R_{\rm DM}) = 3M_{\rm bar}(<R_{\rm DM})/(4\pi R_{\rm DM}^3) = 3g_{\rm crit}/(4\pi G R_{\rm DM})$ yields $\bar\rho_{\rm trans} = 0.068\,M_\odot\text{ pc}^{-3}$ ($4.6 \times 10^{-24}\text{ g cm}^{-3}$; $0.030\,M_\odot\text{ pc}^{-3}$ uncoupled) at the median resolved SPARC crossing radius ($R_{\rm DM} = 2.6\text{ kpc}$) and $\bar\rho_{\rm trans} = 0.50\text{--}0.67\,M_\odot\text{ pc}^{-3}$ ($\sim 3\text{--}4 \times 10^{-23}\text{ g cm}^{-3}$) across the inner 3-D disk scale height ($R \approx 0.26\text{ kpc}$). Crucially, constant-density and constant-acceleration scaling are distinct regimes rather than interchangeable identities: inside rising 3-D baryonic cores ($M_{\rm bar} < M_{\rm cross}^{\rm SPARC} = 1.45 \times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$), the characteristic core density set by $\rho_{\rm core} \sim \Sigma_*/(2h_z) \approx 0.5\,M_\odot\text{ pc}^{-3}$ yields constant-density scaling $R_{\rm DM} = [3M_{\rm enc}/(4\pi\rho_{\rm core})]^{1/3} \propto M^{1/3}$ (observed $\alpha_{\rm rising} = 0.256 \pm 0.069$), whereas in concentrated disks ($M_{\rm bar} > M_{\rm cross}^{\rm SPARC}$) where the crossing occurs in the outer 2-D Keplerian tail at fixed acceleration $g_{\rm crit} = 1.031 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m s}^{-2}$, $M_{\rm enc} = g_{\rm crit}R_{\rm DM}^2/G$ yields constant-acceleration scaling $R_{\rm DM} \propto M^{1/2}$ (observed $\alpha_{\rm tail} = 0.581 \pm 0.086$).

Connection to the MOND Acceleration Scale

The fitted screening density implies a characteristic transition acceleration. At the transition radius, the gravitational acceleration is:

$$g_{\rm TEP} = \frac{GM}{R_{\rm trans}^2} \approx 5 \times 10^{-10}\,{\rm m/s}^2.$$

This is within a factor of 4 of the MOND acceleration scale $a_0 \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-10}\,{\rm m/s}^2$ (Milgrom 1983). The near-coincidence suggests that the TEP screening mechanism and the empirical MOND phenomenology may share a common origin: both describe the transition from screened (Newtonian) to unscreened (modified) gravity at a characteristic acceleration scale set by cosmological boundary conditions.

Connection to the Radial Acceleration Relation

The SPARC database is the source of the celebrated Radial Acceleration Relation (RAR), which shows a tight empirical coupling between observed acceleration $g_{\rm obs}$ and baryonic acceleration $g_{\rm bar}$ (McGaugh et al. 2016). The RAR has been interpreted as evidence for modified gravity (MOND) or as a consequence of dark matter halo profiles fine-tuned to baryonic distributions.

The TEP framework offers a third interpretation: the RAR may emerge from the environment-dependent screening of the proper-time field. At high accelerations (high densities), screening is complete and $g_{\rm obs} \approx g_{\rm bar}$. At low accelerations (low densities), the unscreened time-field gradient contributes additional apparent gravity, producing the characteristic upturn in the RAR. The $M^{1/3}$ scaling of the transition radius is the spatial manifestation of this acceleration-dependent screening.

A fixed-normalization test applies the existing alternative low-gradient kinetic realization directly to the SPARC mass models (pipeline steps 10–13). Its response $P_{,X}(\xi)=k\sqrt{\xi}+2\xi$, with $\xi=|X|/\Lambda_X^4$, differs from the unit-term baseline $1+2\xi$ of Paper 0 §7 and yields the derived flat-tail and baryonic Tully–Fisher limits. With the cosmic temporal-gradient threshold fixed by the Hubble clock roll at $g_t=cH_0/2\simeq3.40\times10^{-10}$ m/s$^2$ and the DEF coupling normalization $\alpha_0=\sqrt{2}\beta_A=-\sqrt{2}$, the natural two-branch crossover $k=16$ ($\xi_*=(k/2)^2=64$) yields $a_{\rm eff}=4\sqrt{2}\,g_t/k=cH_0/(4\sqrt{2})=1.202\times10^{-10}$ m/s$^2$ ($k=4\sqrt{2}\,g_t/a_0\simeq16.03$ at the canonical $a_0=1.20\times10^{-10}$ m/s$^2$), deriving Milgrom's $a_0\sim cH_0$ coincidence from the cosmological time field with no galaxy-specific parameter adjusted across the 175-galaxy sample. The algebraic flux law $\bar{u}P_{,X}(\bar{u}^2/2) = g_{\rm bar}/g_t$ (with canonical zero-gradient regulator $P_{,X}(\xi)=\epsilon_{\rm reg}+k\sqrt{\xi}+2\xi$, $\epsilon_{\rm reg}=10^{-3}$ ensuring strict hyperbolicity at $X=0$ while leaving galactic scales $\xi\sim10^{-2}$–$1$ invariant to $<10^{-4}$) yields the temporal-shear acceleration $a_\phi = 2g_t\bar{u}$, where $k=4\sqrt{2}\,g_t/a_0\simeq16.03$ is calibrated to the empirical scale $a_0=1.20\times10^{-10}$ m/s$^2$ (so agreement of the deep-tail normalization with $a_0$ is a calibration input rather than an independent prediction, while the shape across the 175-galaxy sample has zero refitted parameters). From the point-particle action $L_{\rm pp}=-mc\sqrt{-\tilde g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu}$, the action-derived radial force law is additive, $g_{\rm obs} = g_{\rm bar} + a_\phi$. In the causal matter metric $\tilde g_{\mu\nu} = -A_{\rm loc}^2(u_{\rm grav})U_\mu U_\nu + A_{\rm sp}^2\Pi_{\mu\nu}$, evaluating the dielectric Gauss–Klein–Gordon response on the coupled matter-frame potential gradient ($a_\phi(a_\phi + g_{\rm bar}) = g_{\rm bar}a_{\rm eff}$; Paper 0 §7, Paper 22 §6) yields the closed-form two-branch shear acceleration $a_\phi(r) = \sqrt{(g_{\rm bar}/2)^2 + g_{\rm bar}a_{\rm eff}} - g_{\rm bar}/2$, so $g_{\rm obs} = \tfrac{1}{2}g_{\rm bar} + \sqrt{(g_{\rm bar}/2)^2 + g_{\rm bar}a_{\rm eff}}$ (which also fixes the theoretical velocity ratio at $g_{\rm bar} = a_{\rm eff}$ to $V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm bar} = \sqrt{(1+\sqrt{5})/2} = 1.272 \approx 1.3$). Evaluated at the canonical SPARC mass-to-light ratios ($\Upsilon_d = 0.50, \Upsilon_b = 0.70$) and $k = 16.03$ with zero free parameters, this coupled-gradient additive law achieves an RMS log-residual of $0.1330$ dex ($61.1\%$ within $0.1$ dex, median $-0.010$ dex) on the standard quality-controlled SPARC RAR sample ($N = 2{,}700$ points; matching the empirical MOND interpolating benchmark $0.1328$ dex, $60.1\%$ within $0.1$ dex, to $0.0002$ dex) and $0.1994$ dex ($53.9\%$ within $0.1$ dex vs. MOND $0.1989$ dex, $53.2\%$) across all $3{,}389$ unfiltered table points, while on the Step 13 outer-edge BTFR ($N = 405$ points across $135$ galaxies) it yields median $v_{\rm obs}/v_{\rm pred} = 0.956$ and RMS $0.0807$ dex. By contrast, if $P_{,X}(\bar u^2/2)$ is evaluated using only the uncoupled scalar self-gradient $\bar u^2$ ($a_\phi \simeq \sqrt{g_{\rm bar}a_{\rm eff}}$, omitting the $-g_{\rm bar}/2$ cross-term), direct addition $g_{\rm bar} + a_\phi$ carries a $+2g_{\rm bar}a_\phi$ crossover offset at $\Upsilon_d = 0.50$ ($0.1522$ dex quality-controlled, $0.2185$ dex unfiltered, $k_{\rm fit} \approx 30.9$; absorbed to $0.1392$ and $0.2004$ dex at $\Upsilon_d = 0.35$, or $0.1394$ and $0.1974$ dex under the quadrature comparator $\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}^2 + a_\phi^2}$). A field-value coupling $K(\phi) = e^{c\psi}$ improves the RAR match monotonically (rms $\to 0.199$ dex at $c = 3$) but is excluded by a sign tension with the wide-binary pair channel, which requires the opposite sign, so the monotone form is not adopted. The residual offset is quantified by the accompanying scans: disk mass-to-light $\Upsilon_d = 0.35$ alone absorbs the offset (rms $0.2004$ dex, median $-0.020$ dex — indistinguishable from the MOND reference at zero additional sector parameters); the ambient-shear scan bounds the cosmic-web background at $u_{\rm amb} \lesssim 0.02$ (larger values degrade the deep tail monotonically), validating the isolated-source assumption; and a small canonical term $\epsilon \lesssim 0.1$ in $P_{,X} = \epsilon + k\sqrt{\xi} + 2\xi$ is observationally free and marginally preferred (rms $0.2098$ dex at $\epsilon = 0.1$), while order-unity values are excluded not by the data but by the flat-asymptote requirement — a linear tail reappears below $g_{\rm bar} \sim (\epsilon/k)\,g_t$. The offset is therefore a baryonic mass-model systematic, not a sector-shape failure. Note that the $0.1330$ dex RMS on the quality-controlled $N = 2{,}700$ sample ($0.1994$ dex across all $3{,}389$ raw points; $0.139$–$0.152$ dex and $0.200$–$0.218$ dex for the uncoupled/quadrature comparators) reported here is obtained at fixed global mass-to-light ratios ($\Upsilon_d = 0.35$ or $0.50$, $\Upsilon_b = 0.70$) and zero per-galaxy refitted parameters; by contrast, SPARC RAR analyses that marginalize over individual galaxy mass-to-light ratios, distances, and inclinations (e.g., Li et al. 2018, who obtain $\sim 0.057$ dex residual scatter after fitting three nuisance parameters per galaxy, or Desmond 2023, who jointly infers the intrinsic RAR scatter and galaxy-level systematics) absorb the galaxy-to-galaxy $\Upsilon_d$ variance that remains unadjusted in the present zero-refit test.

Linking RBH-1 to Galactic Halos

The SPARC analysis identifies a cross-scale consistency between the RBH-1 wake phenomenon and galactic dark matter halos:

  • RBH-1 represents a "naked saturation"—a Temporal Topology saturation not anchored to a baryonic host, propagating freely through the intergalactic medium and inducing star formation via its time-dilation wake.
  • Galactic halos represent "baryon-anchored solitons"—the same temporal field structure, but centered on and shaped by the baryonic mass distribution of the host galaxy.

Both phenomena are consistent with the same $M^{1/3}$ scaling law, calibrated from terrestrial GNSS observations. The universality of this scaling across planetary, stellar, galactic, and cosmological mass scales constitutes a central theoretical consequence of the Temporal Equivalence Principle.

Residual Analysis: Discriminating Baryonic Feedback vs Field Theory

The RMS scatter of 0.48 dex around the $M^{1/3}$ relation indicates substantial galaxy-to-galaxy variation. A critical test distinguishes whether this scatter arises from baryonic feedback processes (Standard Model) or from the intrinsic field theory mechanism (TEP): correlate the residuals with baryonic properties versus screening proxies.

Methodology

For each of the 168 SPARC galaxies with valid mass discrepancy radii, the residual from the $M^{1/3}$ expectation is calculated as:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:residual} \text{Residual} = \log_{10}(R_{\rm DM} / R_{\rm exp}) \end{equation}

where $R_{\rm exp} = k \cdot M_{\rm bar}^{1/3}$ with $k = 7.96 \times 10^{-4}$ kpc/$M_\odot^{1/3}$. These residuals are then correlated with:

  • Baryonic properties. Gas fraction ($f_{\rm gas}$), surface brightness ($\Sigma$), and inclination, treated as proxies for feedback efficiency, star formation history, and observational systematics.
  • Screening proxy. Central density ($\rho_{\rm central}$), treated as a proxy for local screening strength independent of $R_{\rm DM}$.

Results

Interpretation

When evaluated using the measured SPARC Table 1 effective radii $R_{\rm eff}$, inclinations $i$, and signed gas contributions (step_7_sparc_residuals.json, $n = 168$), none of the baryonic or observational properties exhibits a strong correlation with the scaling residuals ($\max |r| = 0.153$): inclination ($r = +0.096$, $p = 0.217$) and surface brightness ($r = +0.047$, $p = 0.543$) are statistically null, while gas fraction shows only a weak nominal trend ($r = -0.153$, $p = 0.048$, $|r| < 0.2$) that does not survive Bonferroni correction across the four tested comparisons ($p_{\rm adj} = 0.191$). This confirms that the scaling relation is not driven by rotation-curve deprojection systematics or baryonic feedback efficiency.

The central density ($r = +0.016$, $p = 0.834$) likewise shows no residual trend after the primary mass scaling is removed, consistent with the full rotation-curve radial-acceleration fit in Steps 10 and 13 where the two-branch profile $P_{,X}=\epsilon_{\rm reg}+k\sqrt\xi+2\xi$ ($k=16.03$, $\epsilon_{\rm reg}=10^{-3}$) accounts for the complete radial profile under the coupled-gradient additive law at $0.1330$ dex RMS ($61.1\%$ within $0.1$ dex at canonical $\Upsilon_d=0.50$, vs MOND $0.1328$ dex; uncoupled additive $0.1522$ dex at $\Upsilon_d=0.50$, $0.1392$ dex at $\Upsilon_d=0.35$, and $0.1394$ dex quadrature) on the quality-controlled $N=2{,}700$ SPARC RAR sample and $0.1994$ dex ($53.9\%$ within $0.1$ dex at $\Upsilon_d=0.50$; uncoupled $0.2185$ dex, $0.2004$ dex at $\Upsilon_d=0.35$, $0.1974$ dex quadrature) across all $3{,}389$ raw points.

Summary: across all 168 SPARC galaxies with measured onset radii, the residuals are uncorrelated with galaxy inclination ($r = +0.096$, $p = 0.217$), surface brightness ($r = +0.047$, $p = 0.543$), and central density ($r = +0.016$, $p = 0.834$), and show only a weak gas-fraction association ($r = -0.153$, $p = 0.048$; $p_{\rm adj} = 0.191$ after four-test correction), showing no significant dependence on the tested inclination, surface-brightness, central-density, or gas-fraction proxies after four-test correction.

Scatter Quantification and Comparative Analysis

The observed scatter of $\sigma = 0.48$ dex around the $M^{1/3}$ relation is substantial (corresponding to a factor of $\sim 3$ variation). Quantifying the origin of this scatter is essential for distinguishing the TEP signal from competing hypotheses.

Intrinsic vs. Measurement Scatter

Standard error budgeting suggests that a significant fraction of this scatter is observational. Distance uncertainties in the SPARC sample typically range from 10–20%, which propagate to a ~0.1–0.2 dex uncertainty in $R_{\rm DM}$. Inclination corrections and non-circular motions contribute additional noise. However, the residual scatter ($\sim 0.3$ dex) likely reflects intrinsic variation in the galaxy population.

Comparison with MOND and LCDM+Feedback

MOND: The Modified Newtonian Dynamics framework typically yields a tighter scatter ($\sim 0.13$ dex) in the Radial Acceleration Relation (RAR). This is expected because MOND modifies gravity based on instantaneous local acceleration, a continuous variable. In contrast, this analysis extracts a single discrete parameter ($R_{\rm DM}$) from each rotation curve, a method inherently more sensitive to local irregularities and noise than the integrated RAR analysis. The larger scatter in $R_{\rm DM}$ does not invalidate the mean scaling; rather, it indicates that the "onset radius" is a noisy estimator of the underlying screening transition.

LCDM + Feedback (Null Hypothesis): The standard cosmological model relies on baryonic feedback to explain rotation curve diversity. In this scenario, the "dark matter onset" is not a fundamental scale but an emergent property of halo assembly and feedback history. Hydrodynamic simulations (e.g., NIHAO, FIRE) typically predict a scaling of $R_{\rm DM} \propto M^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha \approx 0.3$–$0.4$, broadly consistent with observation. However, they offer no account of why the galactic relation shares its structural exponent with the compact-object scaling. The fitted core normalization ($\rho_{\rm trans} \approx 0.5\,M_\odot/{\rm pc}^3 \sim 3\times10^{-23}$ g/cm³ at inner core radii, and $\bar\rho_{\rm trans} = 0.068\,M_\odot/{\rm pc}^3$ [$0.030\,M_\odot/{\rm pc}^3$ uncoupled] at the median crossing radius $R_{\rm DM} \approx 2.6\text{ kpc}$) follows directly from the kinetic sector's column-density and coupled-gradient acceleration scale ($g_{\rm crit} = 1.031 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m s}^{-2}, \Sigma_* = 582\,M_\odot\text{ pc}^{-2}$; Eq. [\ref{eq:profile_link}], Step 14), cleanly separating the kinetic halo onset scale $\Lambda_X = \sqrt{M_{\rm Pl}H_0}$ from the compact potential saturation density $\rho_T \approx 20\text{ g/cm}^3$.

Distinctive Cross-Scale Linkage

While MOND provides a tighter fit to galaxy kinematics alone, it offers no explanation for the GNSS clock correlations ($L_c \approx 4200$ km). LCDM+Feedback can accommodate the galaxy scaling but treats the normalization as a free parameter, offering no predictive link to other scales. TEP is distinctive in that its two-branch action simultaneously governs the terrestrial/compact saturation scale ($\rho_T \approx 20\text{ g/cm}^3$ via the quartic potential $V(u)$) and derives the galactic transition density ($\bar\rho_{\rm trans} = 3g_{\rm crit}/(4\pi G R_{\rm DM}) \simeq 0.03\text{--}0.5\,M_\odot/{\rm pc}^3$ and the $M^{1/3} \to M^{1/2}$ crossover mass $M_{\rm cross} = 1.45 \times 10^{10}\,M_\odot$ via the kinetic operator $P_{,X}(\xi)$; Eq. [\ref{eq:profile_link}], Steps 8 and 14) with zero galaxy-specific free parameters.

Methodological Details

The "onset radius" $R_{\rm DM}$ is defined as the first radial bin where $V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm bar} > 1.3$ (fiducial threshold). Specifically:

  1. For each galaxy, the rotation curve is evaluated at the native SPARC radial sampling (not interpolated).
  2. The velocity ratio $V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm bar}$ is computed at each radius.
  3. $R_{\rm DM}$ is the radius of the first bin exceeding the threshold.
  4. Galaxies with non-monotonic or noisy $V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm bar}$ profiles are retained; the "first crossing" definition is robust to subsequent fluctuations.
  5. Galaxies that never exceed the threshold (7 of 175) are excluded from the fit.

The headline value $\alpha_{\rm SPARC}=0.352\pm0.043$ is the threshold-marginalized bootstrap estimate, obtained by resampling galaxies while marginalizing over the accepted onset definitions. The fiducial ($V_{\rm obs}/V_{\rm bar}>1.3$) definition alone gives $0.369\pm0.041$; the difference is retained as onset-definition systematic uncertainty. Varying the threshold from 1.1 to 1.5 yields exponents from 0.27 to 0.41, bracketing the headline estimate.
