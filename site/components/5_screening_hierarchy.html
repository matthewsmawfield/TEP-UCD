4. Temporal Topology Screening Analysis

If a scalar field permeates spacetime, why has it not been detected in precision gravitational experiments? This section answers that question. The key insight is that the scalar field's influence depends on the geometric overlap of topological charge cores: in environments where charge cores are tightly packed, the field is suppressed—a phenomenon called Temporal Topology screening. Rather than invoking discrete thin-shell boundaries, screening operates via the continuous spatial profile of the scalar field (Temporal Topology). The tight geometric packing in deep potential wells suppresses the local field gradient (Temporal Shear), ensuring short-range Temporal Shear suppression while leaving the field light cosmologically. General Relativity is recovered in the regimes where it has been tested most stringently.

The screening factor $S$ quantifies this suppression. It is defined as the ratio of the Temporal Topology saturation radius (where the scalar field saturates) to the physical radius of the object:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:screening_factor} S = \frac{R_T}{R_{phys}}. \end{equation}

The quantity $S$ is used here as a geometric proxy for how deeply the baryonic source is embedded within a saturated soliton configuration. When $S \gg 1$, the physical object occupies a small region relative to the saturated field scale; in this regime the phenomenology assumes that non-linear response in the scalar sector suppresses gradients in the dense interior, recovering GR to high precision in local dynamics. When $S \sim 1$, the system lies near the transition between strongly screened and weakly screened behavior. When $S \ll 1$, the Temporal Topology saturation scale is smaller than the object and the saturated region does not envelop the full baryonic configuration; in this regime the model does not assume strong Temporal Topology suppression a priori, and constraints must be assessed case-by-case.

\begin{equation} \label{eq:ppn_suppression} \alpha_{\rm PPN}^{\rm eff}=\alpha_0 F(S,\rho,\Phi,\nabla\phi), \qquad F\rightarrow 0 \end{equation}

in the screened or source-charge-suppressed limit. The geometric factor $S$ is not itself the observable PPN coupling. Precision tests constrain $\alpha_{\rm PPN}^{\rm eff}$; $S$ is used here only as a proxy for the nonlinear suppression regime.

The near-transition regime $S \sim 0.4$–$0.7$ is resolved by the corpus's channel-projection structure (Paper 0). The screening response is not a single function of $S$: clock-comparison and transport observables probe the amplitude projection $S_A$, while orbital and geodesic observables probe the source-charge/shear projection $\mathcal S_\Sigma$, which is suppressed more strongly for the pairwise configurations used in ephemeris tests. The same geometric $S$ therefore places different channels on different sides of their respective thresholds: Earth's $S \approx 0.66$ admits a detectable clock-covariance signal ($C_A$ covariance channel, the GNSS calibration of Section 2) while the LLR, Cassini, and MESSENGER orbital observables at $S = 0.42$–$0.65$ remain GR-consistent through the $\mathcal S_\Sigma$ projection. The reading of sub-unity $S$ is uniform throughout this paper: $S < 1$ denotes that the saturation region does not envelop the object, i.e. the unscreened side of the transition; the observable amplitude in any given channel is then set by that channel's projection, not by $S$ alone.

4.1 The White Dwarf Stress Test

White Dwarfs are the ideal stress test for two reasons. 4.2 The Empirical Screening Law

Extending this analysis across 26 astrophysical objects (planets, brown dwarfs, main sequence stars, white dwarfs, neutron stars, and binary pulsars) yields a compact summary of how the defined screening factor varies with density. \begin{equation} \label{eq:screening_law} S \equiv \frac{R_T(M)}{R_{\rm phys}} \equiv \left(\frac{\bar\rho}{\rho_T}\right)^{1/3}. \end{equation}

Because $R_T(M) = (3M/4\pi\rho_T)^{1/3}$ and $R_{\rm phys} = (3M/4\pi\bar\rho)^{1/3}$, the $\bar\rho^{1/3}$ dependence is an exact algebraic consequence of the definition of $S$, not an independent statistical fit (an empirical log-log check across the 11 dense objects with $\bar\rho > \rho_T$ recovers $\beta_{\rm scr} = 0.3338 \pm 0.0008$, which is true by definition up to published table rounding). Because each object's density is evaluated at its physical radius, $\bar\rho = 3M/(4\pi R_{\rm phys}^3)$, the diagnostic carries an enclosed-acceleration equivalent: the criterion $S\sim1$ corresponds to $GM/R_{\rm phys}^2 \propto \bar\rho\,R_{\rm phys}$, so the density and acceleration readings of the hierarchy agree object-by-object. Under the stated definitions, this is the expected scaling: if $R_T \propto M^{1/3}$ and $R_{phys} \propto (M/\rho)^{1/3}$, then:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:screening_derivation} S = \frac{R_T}{R_{phys}} = \frac{M^{1/3}}{M^{1/3}/\rho^{1/3}} = \rho^{1/3}. \end{equation}

Conversely, in low-mass uncompressed solid bodies where central gravitational pressure $P_c \ll K_0 \sim 100\text{ GPa}$ — including the six major satellites with measured moments of inertia ($P_c = 2.7\text{--}5.8\text{ GPa}$: Europa $2k_{\rm MOI}/S = 1.30$, Io $1.35$, Ganymede $1.36$, Moon $1.43$, Titan $1.50$, Callisto $1.57$), Mars ($P_c = 24.9\text{ GPa}$, $1.26$, vs. $141\text{--}173\text{ GPa}$ for Venus and Earth), and the minor planets ($1.26\text{--}1.57$) — weak self-compression leaves $k_{\rm MOI}$ near the uniform-sphere ceiling ($0.31\text{--}0.39$) while $S \approx 0.45\text{--}0.58$; and in nuclear-burning main-sequence stars such as the Sun ($\bar\rho_\odot = 1.41\text{ g/cm}^3, S_\odot = 0.413, k_{\rm MOI,\odot} \approx 0.070 \ll S_\odot/2$), radiative-core thermal support produces an $n \approx 3$ Eddington core condensation ($\rho_c \approx 153\text{ g/cm}^3 \approx 108\,\bar\rho_\odot$) outside cold condensed-matter hydrostatics. Multiplying Equation (\ref{eq:veq_tep_law}) by Equation (\ref{eq:kmoi_screening}) cancels both $R_{\rm phys}$ and $R_T(M)$ identically, yielding the empirical Wesson angular-momentum relation:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:wesson_tep_law} j_{\rm TEP}(M) = k_{\rm MOI}\,R_{\rm phys}\,v_{\rm eq}^{\rm TEP} = \frac{GM}{2\,v_{\rm gal}}, \qquad J_{\rm TEP}(M) = p_{\rm TEP}\,M^2, \qquad p_{\rm TEP} \equiv \frac{G}{2\,v_{\rm gal}} = 1.517 \times 10^{-15}\text{ cm}^2\text{ g}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}, \end{equation}

corresponding to a dimensionless Kerr-like spin parameter $\chi_{\rm TEP} \equiv cJ/(GM^2) = c/(2v_{\rm gal}) = 1/(2\sqrt{u_{\rm gal}}) = 681.3$. Crucially, for any body, the exact algebraic identity $$\frac{J_{\rm obs}}{J_{\rm TEP}} \equiv \left(\frac{2\,k_{\rm MOI}}{S}\right) \left(\frac{v_{\rm eq}^{\rm obs}}{v_{\rm eq}^{\rm TEP}}\right)$$ holds by definition: although $\rho_T$ cancels out of the product $J_{\rm TEP}(M)$, measuring $k_{\rm MOI}$ and $v_{\rm eq}$ separately constrains $\rho_T \approx 20\text{ g/cm}^3$ through both factors simultaneously ($2k_{\rm MOI}/S \propto \rho_T^{1/3}$ and $v_{\rm obs}/v_{\rm TEP} \propto \rho_T^{-1/3}$—an unadjusted $\rho_T \sim 220\text{ g/cm}^3$ would shift both factors by $2.23\times$ in opposite directions, to $2.61$ and $0.45$). Inverting Equation (\ref{eq:veq_tep_law}) directly via $\rho_T^{\rm spin} = \frac{3}{4\pi}(v_{\rm eq}^{\rm obs}v_{\rm gal}/GM^{2/3})^3 = \rho_T(v_{\rm obs}/v_{\rm TEP})^3$ across the four unsaturated ambient-coupled planets yields individual spin-implied saturation densities of $24.1\text{ g/cm}^3$ (Earth), $19.6\text{ g/cm}^3$ (Uranus, using the 2025 HST auroral rotation period $17\text{ h }14\text{ m }52\text{ s} = 17.247864 \pm 0.000010\text{ hr}$, Lamy et al. 2025), $15.7\text{ g/cm}^3$ (Neptune), and $25.5\text{ g/cm}^3$ (Saturn), with a geometric mean of $\rho_T^{\rm spin} = 20.9\text{ g/cm}^3$ ($21.2 \pm 4.4\text{ g/cm}^3$ arithmetic mean; and $21.1\text{ g/cm}^3$ geometric mean across the nine planetary and substellar systems under the one-parameter $\eta_0 = 0.129$ two-branch model), while inverting Equation (\ref{eq:kmoi_screening}) via $\rho_T^{\rm MOI} = \bar\rho/(8k_{\rm MOI}^3)$ yields $\rho_T^{\rm MOI,\text{terr}} = 17.9 \pm 1.4\text{ g/cm}^3$ sample scatter across the three iron-core terrestrial planets ($19.1\text{ g/cm}^3$ for Earth, $18.2\text{ g/cm}^3$ for Venus with $\pm 20\%$ propagated $k_{\rm MOI}$ uncertainty, $16.4\text{ g/cm}^3$ for Mercury) and $\rho_T^{\rm MOI} = 13.7 \pm 4.2\text{ g/cm}^3$ across all eight planets, exhibiting a systematic trend with planet composition from iron-core terrestrials ($16.4\text{--}19.1\text{ g/cm}^3$) to ice giants ($14.8\text{ g/cm}^3$ for Neptune, $13.9\text{ g/cm}^3$ for Uranus), low-pressure Mars ($10.0\text{ g/cm}^3$), and compressible H/He gas giants ($9.0\text{ g/cm}^3$ for Jupiter, $8.3\text{ g/cm}^3$ for Saturn). Across the five ambient-coupled major planets (Earth, Uranus, Neptune, Saturn, and Jupiter), the observed spin angular momenta match the zero-parameter Wesson relation $p_{\rm TEP} = G/(2v_{\rm gal})$ at $J_{\rm obs}/J_{\rm TEP} = 1.11 \pm 0.25$ ($\bar\chi_{\rm obs} = 758.7 \pm 169.0$ versus $\chi_{\rm TEP} = 681.3$).

A necessary condition separating the ambient Galactic Wesson branch ($J \propto M^2$) from the low-mass intrinsic soliton-clock branch ($j \propto M^{2/3}$) is obtained by comparing each body's two-branch screening shell radius $r_*(M) = \sqrt{GM/g_t}$ ($g_t = cH_0/2 = 3.40 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m s}^{-2}$; Paper 0 §7) to its heliocentric orbital semimajor axis $a$ (Table 4.3). Unbraked planets whose screening shell reaches their orbit ($r_*(M)/a \gtrsim 1$, with Neptune at the boundary) can couple to the Galactic shear floor: Jupiter ($r_*/a = 24.79$, $J_{\rm obs}/J_{\rm TEP} = 0.824$), Saturn ($r_*/a = 7.37$, $J_{\rm obs}/J_{\rm TEP} = 1.505$), Earth ($r_*/a = 7.24$, $J_{\rm obs}/J_{\rm TEP} = 1.083$), Uranus ($r_*/a = 1.44$, $J_{\rm obs}/J_{\rm TEP} = 1.129$), and Neptune ($r_*(M_{\rm Nep}) = 29.97\text{ AU}$ versus $a_{\rm Nep} = 30.11\text{ AU}$, $r_*/a = 0.995$, $J_{\rm obs}/J_{\rm TEP} = 1.026$), whereas Mars ($r_*/a = 1.56$, $\chi_{\rm obs} = 2094.6$, $\eta_{\rm rot} = 0.033$) sits in the transition zone between the two branches. As a secondary four-planet diagnostic on the ambient branch, this nested heliocentric screening-shell ratio $r_*(M)/a$ also orders the opposite signs of the individual spin residuals around Eq. (\ref{eq:veq_tep_law}): because $\sqrt{M_{\rm Sat}/M_\oplus} = 9.755 \approx a_{\rm Sat}/a_\oplus = 9.582$, Earth ($r_*/a = 7.24$, $+6.4\%$) and Saturn ($r_*/a = 7.37$, $+8.4\%$) share nearly identical nested screening-shell ratios deep in the $r_*/a \gg 1$ regime and rotate faster than the baseline formula by the same margin ($+6.3\%$ and $+4.7\%$ at Saturn's oblateness-corrected volumetric radius $\bar R_{\rm Sat} = 58{,}232\text{ km}$, and $+5.7\%$ under the $10.561\text{ hr}$ Cassini ring-seismology deep-interior period), whereas Uranus ($r_*/a = 1.44$, $-0.6\%$) and Neptune ($r_*/a = 0.995$, $-7.7\%$) sit at the $r_*/a \to 1$ boundary and rotate slightly slower (correlating with $\ln[r_*(M)/a]$ across the four ambient planets at Pearson $r = +0.960, p = 0.0398$, Spearman $\rho = +1.000$, reducing residual scatter from $\pm 7.3\%$ to $\pm 1.81\%$, and yielding $\bar v_{\rm rot,int}/v_{\rm TEP} = 1.031 \pm 0.041$, $\rho_T^{\rm spin,vol} = 21.9\text{ g/cm}^3$ under volumetric radii and deep-interior rotation periods). Conversely, for small bodies whose screening shell is much smaller than their orbital radius ($r_*(M)/a \ll 1$: Hygiea $0.009$, Pallas $0.015$, Vesta $0.020$, Ceres $0.033$, Quaoar $0.002$, and Haumea $0.004$), rotation decouples from the Galactic floor and follows the intrinsic soliton-clock capacity $j_{\rm sol}(M) = \eta_{\rm rot}\,R_T(M)^2\,\omega_T \propto M^{2/3}$ (six-body median $\eta_{\rm med} = 0.138$, interquartile range $\eta_{\rm IQR} \in [0.122,\,0.154]$, mean $0.157 \pm 0.076$, and $\eta_0 = 0.129$ at Ceres), yielding a log-log slope of $0.941 \pm 0.059$ ($R^2 = 0.9735$, $p = 8.85 \times 10^{-7}$) against $u_T(M)$ across seven orders of magnitude in mass (Figure 6). Notably, the combined Earth+Moon system ($r_*/a = 7.28$, $T_{\rm prim} = 5.05\text{ hr}$, $\eta_{\rm E+M} = 0.145$, or $0.119$ at fixed $k_{\rm MOI}$) sits inside the small-body soliton capacity interquartile range following the Moon-forming giant impact, while subsequent and still-ongoing lunar tidal dissipation ($\dot{a}_{\rm Moon} \approx +3.8\text{ cm yr}^{-1}$, lengthening the day by $\sim 2\text{ ms century}^{-1}$) has spun Earth down across the intermediate region to its present-day epochal value near the Wesson line ($\chi_{\rm obs} = 737.8$ vs. $\chi_{\rm TEP} = 681.3$, a secular transit rather than a static tidal equilibrium). Extending this soliton-capacity branch out of sample beyond the Solar System to super-Jupiters and ultra-cool brown dwarfs ($M > M_{\rm cap} = 0.61\,M_{\rm Jup}$; Snellen et al. 2014; Gagné et al. 2017; Bryan et al. 2020; Tannock et al. 2021) exposes a direct out-of-sample test of both $R_T(M)$ and the clock-amplitude saturation ceiling $S_A(\bar\rho) = \min[1,\,(\bar\rho/\rho_T)^{1/3}] = \min[1,\,S]$ (Paper 0 §3). Because the active saturation core inside a body has radius $R_{\rm active} = S_A\,R_{\rm phys} = \min[R_T(M),\,R_{\rm phys}]$, the maximum specific angular momentum is $j_{\rm cap}(M,\,R_{\rm phys}) = \eta_0\,\min[R_T(M),\,R_{\rm phys}]^2\,\omega_T$, which defines a dimensionless Keplerian breakup-fraction ceiling $v_{\rm cap}/v_{\rm breakup} = (\eta_0/k_{\rm MOI})\sqrt{\min(1,\,S)}$ across two high-mass regimes above $M_{\rm cap}$: (i) for unscreened planetary-mass objects with $\bar\rho < \rho_T = 20\text{ g/cm}^3$ ($S < 1$, $M \lesssim 16\,M_{\rm Jup}$), $R_{\rm active} = R_T(M) < R_{\rm phys}$ and the rotational ceiling rises as $v_{\rm cap}(M) = \eta_0 R_T(M)^2\omega_T/(k_{\rm MOI}R_{\rm phys})$ — predicting $v_{\rm cap} = 64.9\text{ km s}^{-1}$ ($P_{\rm cap} = 2.35\text{ hr}$, $v_{\rm cap}/v_{\rm breakup} = 0.478$) for the fastest-rotating planetary-mass object known, SIMP J013656.5+093347 ($M = 12.7 \pm 1.0\,M_{\rm Jup}, R = 1.22\,R_{\rm Jup}, \bar\rho = 8.67\text{ g/cm}^3, S = 0.76, k_{\rm MOI} = 0.235$; Gagné et al. 2017), matching its observed $v_{\rm eq} = 63.1\text{ km s}^{-1}$ ($P_{\rm obs} = 2.414\text{ hr}, v_{\rm obs}/v_{\rm breakup} = 0.464$, $v_{\rm obs}/v_{\rm cap} = 0.972$, within $-2.8\%$), while young contracting exoplanets such as $\beta$ Pictoris b ($11\,M_{\rm Jup}, v\sin i = 25\text{ km s}^{-1}$, deprojected $v_{\rm eq} \approx 31.8\text{ km s}^{-1}$ vs. $v_{\rm cap} = 46.6\text{ km s}^{-1}$; Snellen et al. 2014) and the 27-object sample of Bryan et al. (2020; $v/v_{\rm breakup} \in [0.05,\,0.31]$, $0/27$ exceeding the ceiling) lie well below this upper boundary; and (ii) once $\bar\rho \ge \rho_T = 20\text{ g/cm}^3$ ($S = R_T/R_{\rm phys} \ge 1$, in degenerate brown dwarfs with $M \gtrsim 16\,M_{\rm Jup}$), the active radius saturates at the physical surface ($R_{\rm active} = R_{\rm phys}$, $v_{\rm cap}/v_{\rm breakup} = \eta_0/k_{\rm MOI} = 0.630$), so $R_{\rm phys}$ cancels out of $P_{\min} = 2\pi R_{\rm phys}/v_{\rm cap}$ and locks the minimum rotation period of all degenerate brown dwarfs onto a universal, mass- and radius-independent floor set directly by $\tau_T = 2\pi/\omega_T = 0.7381\text{ hr}$ ($44.3\text{ min}$): $$P_{\min}^{\rm TEP}(\bar\rho \ge \rho_T) = \frac{k_{\rm MOI}}{\eta_0}\,\tau_T = \frac{0.205}{0.129}\,\tau_T = 1.172\text{ hr} \quad (70.3\text{ min}),$$ where $k_{\rm MOI} = 0.205$ is the exact Lane–Emden gyration factor of an $n = 1.5$ degenerate polytrope (propagating the six-body small-body interquartile range $\eta_{\rm IQR} \in [0.122,\,0.154]$ gives $P_{\min}^{\rm TEP} \in [0.98,\,1.24]\text{ hr}$, with $1.10\text{ hr}$ at the six-body median $\eta_{\rm med} = 0.138$).
