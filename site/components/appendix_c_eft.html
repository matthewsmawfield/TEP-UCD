Appendix C: Candidate EFT Realizations of the Soliton Profile

The empirical saturation scale $\rho_T \approx 20$ g/cm³ is established phenomenologically from GNSS clock correlations and SPARC scaling. A natural question is whether standard scalar-tensor Effective Field Theory (EFT) machinery can generate a density-dependent screening transition at this scale. This appendix presents two candidate completions: the symmetron, retained as an external benchmark whose $\mathbb{Z}_2$ mechanism is standard in the modified-gravity literature but whose quadratic coupling is not the corpus's universal exponential; and a confining-potential realization constructed directly on the corpus coupling $A(\phi) = e^{-\phi/M_{\rm Pl}}$ ($\beta_A = -1$), which is the corpus-internal demonstration that the observed phenomenology is mechanistically possible without requiring new physics beyond a self-interacting scalar sector. The completions are benchmark models, not unique derivations; the corpus's working screening realization is the selected two-branch nonlinear kinetic sector of the Paper-0 master action, $P(X,\phi) = -V(\phi) + \frac{2k}{3\Lambda_X^2}X|X|^{1/2} + X|X|/\Lambda_X^4$ (with the unit-term form $P = X - V + X|X|/\Lambda_X^4$ retained as the high-gradient control), whose response $P_{,X}(\xi) = k\sqrt{\xi} + 2\xi$ ($\xi = |X|/\Lambda_X^4$, $k = 16.03$) saturates the scalar coupling once the field gradient exceeds the kinetic scale $\Lambda_X = \sqrt{M_{\rm Pl}H_0} \approx 1.9$ meV (Paper 0, A4, §7; analogous to non-canonical kinetic K-mouflage screening in the scalar-tensor literature, Babichev et al. 2009; Barreira et al. 2015, where precision Solar-System perihelion, Lunar Laser Ranging, and time-varying $G_{\rm eff}$ bounds complement Cassini Shapiro-delay constraints). The kinetic scale fixes the shear threshold $g_t$ — equivalently a surface-density crossover $\Sigma_* \sim 3 M_{\rm Pl}^2 H_0 \approx 0.12$ g/cm² inside sources on the unit-term interior benchmark (step 14) — not a volume-density scale: the density argument of the amplitude sector enters through the potential normalization, and the potential-sector realization below is constructed on the corpus's own quartic coupling. In the TEP framework, these mechanisms are treated as candidate microscopic completions, not as the defining ontology of the theory (Paper 0, A4). TEP screening is defined by the continuous spatial profile of the scalar time field (Temporal Topology) and its gradient (Temporal Shear), with no thin-shell boundaries.

C.1 Symmetron Screening: Density-Triggered Symmetry Breaking

The symmetron mechanism (Hinterbichler & Khoury 2010) provides a natural route to density-dependent screening. The scalar potential is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:symmetron_potential} V(\phi) = -\frac{1}{2}\mu^2 \phi^2 + \frac{1}{4}\lambda \phi^4, \end{equation}

and the conformal coupling to matter is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:symmetron_coupling} A(\phi) = 1 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\phi}{M}\right)^2, \end{equation}

where $M$ is the symmetry-breaking scale. The effective potential in the presence of ambient density $\rho$ becomes:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:symmetron_veff} V_{\rm eff}(\phi) = V(\phi) + \rho \left[A(\phi) - 1\right] = -\frac{1}{2}\mu_{\rm eff}^2 \phi^2 + \frac{1}{4}\lambda \phi^4, \end{equation}

with effective mass parameter:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:symmetron_meff} \mu_{\rm eff}^2 = \mu^2 - \frac{\rho}{M^2}. \end{equation}

The critical behavior is immediate. When $\rho > \rho_* \equiv \mu^2 M^2$, the symmetry-breaking parameter $\mu_{\rm eff}^2 < 0$ turns negative so the physical squared mass (curvature of $V_{\rm eff}$ at the origin, $m_{\rm eff}^2(0) \equiv V_{\rm eff}''(0) = -\mu_{\rm eff}^2 = \rho/M^2 - \mu^2 > 0$) is positive and the field sits stably at the symmetric minimum $\phi = 0$; the scalar is massive and screened, so Temporal Shear effects are suppressed. When $\rho < \rho_*$, symmetry is spontaneously broken, the field rolls to $\phi_0 = \mu_{\rm eff}/\sqrt{\lambda}$, and the scalar becomes light—generating long-range forces. The saturation scale is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:symmetron_rhostar} \rho_* = \mu^2 M^2. \end{equation}

Identifying $\rho_* \equiv \rho_T \approx 20$ g/cm³ fixes one relation between $\mu$ and $M$. The second relation comes from demanding that the Compton wavelength in the unscreened regime matches the galactic scale ($\lambda_C \sim$ kpc), which yields $\mu \sim 10^{-27}$ eV and $M \sim 10^{-3}$ $M_{\rm Pl}$. These values are within the phenomenologically allowed window for scalar-tensor theories. Within the corpus this completion is an external benchmark only: its quadratic coupling $A(\phi) = 1 + \phi^2/2M^2$ is not the universal exponential, and the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ structure on which the mechanism rests cannot be imposed on the linearly coupled exponential $A = e^{-\phi/M_{\rm Pl}}$.

C.2 Confining-Potential Screening on the Corpus Coupling

Under the corpus's frozen coupling $\beta_A = -1$, the matter term enters the effective potential with negative slope, so the runaway inverse-power branch $V \propto \phi^{-n}$ used in chameleon completions is inadmissible: both $V' < 0$ and $\beta_A\rho A/M_{\rm Pl} < 0$ for all $\phi > 0$, no minimum of $V_{\rm eff}$ exists, and the field runs away rather than saturating. The admissible branch is a confining potential, $V' > 0$. Writing the dimensionless field $u = \phi/M_{\rm Pl}$, the admissible family is

\begin{equation} \label{eq:chameleon_potential} V(u) = \frac{\lambda}{m}\,M_{\rm Pl}^4\,u^{m}, \qquad m > 2, \end{equation}

whose coefficient is the dimensionless coupling $\lambda$ — not a fourth-power energy density, and distinct from the GNSS covariance length denoted $\lambda$ in Section 3. The corpus's own matter-hosting branch is the quartic member of this family, the weak-field truncation of the master potential $V(u) = V_{\rm matter}(u)e^{-(u/u_s)^4} + V_0 e^{-(u_s/u)^4}$ (Paper 0, Section 8). The conformal coupling is the corpus exponential:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:chameleon_coupling} A(u) = e^{-u} \qquad (\beta_A = -1). \end{equation}

The effective potential is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:chameleon_veff} V_{\rm eff}(u) = V(u) + \rho\left[A(u) - 1\right] = \frac{\lambda}{m}M_{\rm Pl}^4\,u^m + \rho\left(e^{-u} - 1\right). \end{equation}

Its derivative $V_{\rm eff}' = \lambda M_{\rm Pl}^4 u^{m-1} - \rho\,e^{-u}$ vanishes at

\begin{equation} \label{eq:chameleon_umin} \lambda\,u_{\rm min}^{m-1}\,e^{u_{\rm min}} = \frac{\rho}{M_{\rm Pl}^4} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad u_{\rm min} \approx \left(\frac{\rho}{\lambda M_{\rm Pl}^4}\right)^{1/(m-1)} \;\; (u_{\rm min} \ll 1), \end{equation}

so a minimum exists for every $\rho > 0$, with the field elevated inside matter ($u_{\rm min} > 0$) and approaching zero in the ambient — the direction required by the corpus sign convention. The tracking exponent is then fixed, not free: the quartic potential derives the unclipped equilibrium tracking ratio $S(\bar\rho) \equiv u_{\rm min}(\bar\rho)/u_{\rm min}(\rho_T) = (\bar\rho/\rho_T)^{1/3} = R_T(M)/R_{\rm phys}$ (the $u_{\rm min} \ll 1$ truncation; the full $\lambda u^3 e^u$ solve agrees to $\sim 4\times10^{-10}$ relative, step 14), because reproducing the $(\bar\rho/\rho_T)^{1/3}$ scaling requires $1/(m-1) = 1/3$, i.e. $m = 4$ — the quartic member already adopted by the master potential. Macroscopic observables then project this unclipped ratio $S(\bar\rho) = R_T(M)/R_{\rm phys}$ in two complementary ways across the corpus: (i) for surface rotational and structural kinematics (Section 4.7), because a body's matter distribution terminates at its physical surface $R_{\rm phys}$, the active screened radius enclosed within the body cannot exceed $R_{\rm phys}$ ($R_{\rm active} = \min[R_T(M),\,R_{\rm phys}]$), which imposes the geometric surface ceiling $S_A(\bar\rho) \equiv R_{\rm active}/R_{\rm phys} = \min[1, (\bar\rho/\rho_T)^{1/3}]$ (whereas the local equilibrium $u_{\rm min}(\bar\rho)$ itself continues to grow as $\bar\rho^{1/3}$); and (ii) for deep-interior clock/coupling drift transmission in over-saturated compact objects ($\bar\rho > \rho_T$, Paper 22 §6.1), the unclipped ratio $S_{\rm prog} = u_{\rm min}(\bar\rho)/u_{\rm min}(\rho_T) = (\bar\rho/\rho_T)^{1/3} > 1$ ($S_{\rm WD} \simeq 46.4$ inside Chandrasekhar white dwarfs) measures the interior over-saturation depth and suppresses transmitted cosmological $G_{\rm loc}$ drift by $[\max(1, S_{\rm prog})]^{-1}$. Likewise, in the unified matter metric $\tilde g_{\mu\nu} = -A_{\rm loc}^2(u_{\rm grav}(\mathbf{x}))U_\mu U_\nu + A_{\rm sp}^2(u_{\rm drift}(t,\mathbf{x}))\Pi_{\mu\nu} + B(\phi)(\cdots)$ of Paper 0 §2.2, quasi-static local soliton dynamics at a fixed cosmic epoch $t_0$ ($A_{\rm sp}(t_0) = 1$) probe spatial gradients of the lapse $A_{\rm loc}(\mathbf{x}) = e^{-u_{\rm grav}(\mathbf{x})}$, whereas cosmological radiation propagation along the homogeneous drift background ($A_{\rm loc}(t) = A_{\rm sp}(t) = A(\bar\phi(t))$, $B(\phi)k^\mu\partial_\mu\phi \to 0$; Paper 22 §2.2) specializes to the conformal radiation metric $\tilde g_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\bar\phi)g_{\mu\nu}$ with $c_T \equiv c$ and $\Xi(z) = A_{\rm em}/A_0 = (1+z)^{-1}$. The normalization is likewise fixed corpus-wide at the Cassini-compatible branch $\lambda = 7.5\times10^{-66}$ (LAMBDA_QUARTIC_CASSINI; the fiducial point $\lambda_{\rm ref} = 7.5\times10^{-71}$ of the same family fails the corrected linear-in-$S_\Sigma$ Cassini evaluation by $\sim 250\times$, Paper 0 Appendix E step 03). As an energy scale the potential normalization is $\Lambda_V \equiv \lambda^{1/4}M_{\rm Pl} \approx 1.3\times10^{2}$ GeV — fourteen orders of magnitude above the kinetic scale $\Lambda_X \approx 1.9$ meV, and six orders above the energy-density reading $\rho_T^{1/4} \approx 96$ keV of the saturation density (Section 5.5). These are three distinct quantities: $\Lambda_X$ is the gradient threshold of the kinetic sector, $\lambda$ the amplitude-sector coupling, and $\rho_T$ the geometric reference density of the $R_T(M)$ law. Under this normalization the equilibrium remains deeply perturbative at all matter densities: $u_{\rm min}(\rho_T) = 6.9\times10^{-9}$ and $u_{\rm min}(\bar\rho_\oplus) = 4.5\times10^{-9}$, matching the terrestrial share ($\sim 0.5\%$) of the nested field decomposition (Paper 0, Section 2.2).

On the canonical-kinetic equilibrium benchmark ($P_{,X} \to 1$), the field equation in a static, spherically symmetric environment is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:chameleon_eom} \frac{d^2 u}{dr^2} + \frac{2}{r}\frac{du}{dr} = \frac{1}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}V_{\rm eff}'(u), \end{equation}

On the canonical-kinetic equilibrium benchmark, the effective mass squared around $u_{\rm min}(\rho)$ is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:chameleon_meff} m_{\rm eff}^2 = \frac{1}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}V_{\rm eff}''(u_{\rm min}) = \frac{3\lambda M_{\rm Pl}^4\,u_{\rm min}^{2} + \rho\,e^{-u_{\rm min}}}{M_{\rm Pl}^2} \simeq 3\lambda M_{\rm Pl}^2 u_{\rm min}^{2}, \end{equation}

C.3 The Saturation Scale: Calibration versus Derivation

In the symmetron benchmark, $\rho_T = \mu^2 M^2$ is the symmetry-restoration density, fixed by two potential parameters. On the corpus branch the analogous question — which property of the scalar action returns $\rho_T$ — has a sharper answer than a dimensional guess. Under the corpus normalization the equilibrium at the transition density is $u_{\rm min}(\rho_T) = 6.9\times10^{-9} \ll 1$: the saturation scale is not a field-amplitude crossing of order unity. What $\rho_T$ parameterizes is the geometric constant of the saturation-radius law $R_T(M) = (3M/4\pi\rho_T)^{1/3}$, calibrated by the GNSS covariance length (Section 2); its dynamical proxy on the quartic branch is the Compton-resolution crossover $\lambda_c(\bar\rho) \sim R_T(M)$, which for Earth sits at $\bar\rho \sim 4$ g/cm³ — the terrestrial condensed-matter window in which the measured $\rho_T \approx 20$ g/cm³ resides.

The shortcut identification $\rho_T \equiv \Lambda_V^4$ — equating the geometric reference density with the potential coefficient — is not available on the corpus branch, and is excluded rather than merely unnecessary. Imposing $u_{\rm min}(\rho_T) \sim 1$ is equivalent to fixing $\lambda = \rho_T/M_{\rm Pl}^4 \approx 2.4\times10^{-90}$, and that normalization fails three independent checks (step 14): it lies $3\times10^{24}$ below the Cassini-compatible quartic normalization that Solar-System screening requires; it drives the neutron-star-core equilibrium to $u_{\rm min} \approx 21$, past the master-potential knee $u_s \simeq 10$ and onto the Planck-density floor — observed pulsars are not temporal wells; and its implied Compton length at the transition density, $\lambda_c(\rho_T) \approx 3.4\times10^{7}$ km, exceeds any terrestrial body by more than three orders of magnitude, so the local equilibrium it invokes never forms within the host. The same comparison furnishes a directional lower bound: keeping neutron-star interiors below the knee, $u_{\rm min}(\rho_{\rm NS}) < u_s$ at $\rho_{\rm NS} \sim 2\times10^{14}$ g/cm³, requires $\lambda \gtrsim 10^{-84}$, which the corpus branch satisfies at nineteen orders of margin. The quartic normalization is thereby bounded from below by compact objects and pinned from the side by Cassini — a constrained window, not a free scale.

The correct microscopic reading is therefore: $\rho_T$ is the measured geometric constant of $R_T(M)$; the quartic coupling $\lambda$ is the amplitude-sector normalization; and the order-unity self-quenching crossing sits at $\rho_{\rm sat} \sim \lambda M_{\rm Pl}^4 \approx 6\times10^{25}$ g/cm³, inside the temporal-well regime of Paper 28. Meanwhile, the SPARC galactic rotation curves and outer-edge BTFR are governed directly by the two-branch kinetic sector $P_{,X}=k\sqrt\xi+2\xi$ ($k=16.03$ fixed externally by $a_0$, Steps 10 and 13), which supplies the exact $a_{\rm eff}=a_0$ asymptote with zero per-galaxy free parameters.

C.4 Status and Caveats

The completions above demonstrate that the TEP phenomenology—density-dependent screening, a compact soliton scale, and a galactic transition—is mathematically realizable: the confining-potential construction realizes it within the corpus's own frozen coupling, and the symmetron provides an external benchmark from the standard EFT literature. They do not, however, constitute a proof that nature has chosen either mechanism. Key open questions include:

  • Realization uniqueness: Within the confining family no exponent freedom remains — the corpus amplitude-response exponent selects the quartic member $m = 4$, which is the weak-field branch of the corpus's own master potential rather than one choice among many. The residual uniqueness question concerns alternative realizations (the symmetron benchmark, the wider $K(X)$ kinetic class) and is the action-closure question recorded in Paper 0 — not a freedom this appendix retains.
  • Quantum stability: The small quartic coupling $\lambda \approx 7.5\times10^{-66}$ is radiatively stable in the technical sense — its quantum corrections are proportional to $\lambda$ itself. The $\mu \sim 10^{-27}$ eV mass-scale exposure belongs to the symmetron benchmark, not to the corpus realization.
  • Terrestrial transition width: On the canonical-kinetic quartic branch ($\lambda = 7.53\times 10^{-66}$, $\rho_T = 20$ g/cm³), the 4-layer PREM Earth BVP solve and 3-D Yukawa surface covariance projection (Step 14) recovers a transition gradient centroid at $R_{\rm grad} = 4{,}875$ km ($1.18\,R_T(M_\oplus)$) and a surface-network decay length of $L_c^{\rm PREM} = 4{,}010$ km ($L_c^{\rm vol} = 3{,}213$ km full-volume), while the 100-iteration damped Picard solution of the coupled 1-D spherical nonlinear two-branch operator Eq. ($\ref{eq:nonlinear_chameleon_eom}$) converges to $\|\Delta u^{(100)}\|_\infty/\|u\|_\infty = 2.67 \times 10^{-14}$ and yields $R_{\rm grad}^{\rm NL\text{-}PREM} = 4{,}810$ km ($1.160\,R_T(M_\oplus)$), $L_c^{\rm NL\text{-}PREM} = 3{,}957$ km, and $L_c^{\rm NL,vol} = 3{,}170$ km (Section C.2). Extending this radial nonlinear solve to non-spherical 3-D azimuthal orbital-motion anisotropy remains a target for future work.

The existence of a corpus-internal completion that structurally reproduces the salient phenomenology under the frozen $\beta_A = -1$ coupling — with the symmetron as an external benchmark — removes the objection that $\rho_T \approx 20$ g/cm³ is a numerological construct. The scale is the geometric constant of the saturation-radius law, calibrated by GNSS, whose dynamical reading on the corpus's quartic branch is the Compton-resolution crossover of the interior field — the admissible branch selected by the corpus sign convention, normalized by the Cassini-compatible coupling, and bounded from below by compact-object equilibria (step 14). It is anchored to terrestrial kinematic data at every step.
