Local Causal-Cone and Hyperbolicity Analysis

Objective. Establish that on the specified static background the effective causal (matter) metric $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}$ and the scalar field equation of motion preserve locally Lorentz-invariant matter/photon cones and strict Cauchy hyperbolicity, and that long-range spatial correlations reflect static boundary-value geometry rather than dynamic propagation.

Step 1: The Bimetric Causal Cones

In TEP (Paper 0), gravity is governed by the Einstein-frame metric $g_{\mu\nu}$. Matter and electromagnetic fields evolve along the geodesics of the causal (Jordan-frame) metric, written here on the isotropic conformal–disformal benchmark (in the canonical unified unstretched-space decomposition of Paper 0 §2.2, $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = -A_{\rm loc}^2 U_\mu U_\nu + A_{\rm sp}^2 \Pi_{\mu\nu} + B(\phi)\Pi_\mu^\alpha\Pi_\nu^\beta\nabla_\alpha\phi\nabla_\beta\phi$):

\begin{equation} \label{eq:jordan_metric} \tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\phi) g_{\mu\nu} + B(\phi) \nabla_\mu \phi \nabla_\nu \phi. \end{equation}

To ensure causality, the inverse metric $\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}$ must exist, and the Lorentzian signature $(-, +, +, +)$ must be strictly preserved. By the Sherman–Morrison formula, the inverse is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:inverse_metric} \tilde{g}^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{A^2(\phi)} g^{\mu\nu} - \frac{B(\phi)}{A^2(\phi) \big[A^2(\phi) + B(\phi) (\nabla\phi)^2\big]} \nabla^\mu \phi \nabla^\nu \phi, \end{equation}

where $(\nabla\phi)^2 \equiv g^{\alpha\beta} \nabla_\alpha \phi \nabla_\beta \phi$.

Condition 1 (Signature Preservation). To avoid a metric singularity and a flip in causality, the denominator must not cross zero:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:signature_condition} A^2(\phi) + B(\phi) (\nabla\phi)^2 > 0. \end{equation}

As long as Condition 1 holds on the $B(\phi) \ge 0$, static-gradient benchmark ($\partial_t\phi = 0$, $(\nabla\phi)^2 > 0$), the causal cone of $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}$ remains strictly nested inside, or tangent to, the gravitational light cone of $g_{\mu\nu}$.

Step 2: Signal Velocity of Electromagnetic / Matter Fields

Computing the local speed of light $c_\gamma$ (matter-frame photons) in a static spherically symmetric background. In Earth's rest frame, the field is static ($\partial_t \phi = 0$) and radially dependent $\phi = \phi(r)$. The metric components become:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:metric_components} \tilde{g}_{00} = A^2(\phi) g_{00} = -A^2 c^2, \qquad \tilde{g}_{rr} = A^2(\phi) g_{rr} + B(\phi) (\partial_r \phi)^2. \end{equation}

For a radial null signal in the matter frame, $\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} dx^\mu dx^\nu = 0$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:null_signal} -A^2 c^2 dt^2 + \big[A^2 + B(\phi) (\partial_r \phi)^2\big] dr^2 = 0, \end{equation}

which yields the effective light speed:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:effective_speed} c_\gamma^2 \equiv \left( \frac{dr}{dt} \right)^2 = \frac{A^2 c^2}{A^2 + B(\phi)(\partial_r \phi)^2}. \end{equation}

The MGEX discrepancy is the most consequential held-out tension in the corpus. Paper 14 reports $\lambda \approx 1{,}862 \pm 112$ km (corrected pooled; the original ~1-year headline was 1,396 km) from a combined multi-constellation product, versus the ~4,200 km scale recovered from multi-year single-center analyses. Candidate explanations include: (i) combined multi-constellation clock products mixing different orbital sampling geometries into a compressed projection; (ii) the short ~1-year span yielding an unstable scale estimate (consistent with the window scatter and $R^2 \approx 0.70$ on the pooled fit); or (iii) a genuinely different scale. The pre-registered discriminator is a per-constellation analysis: if GPS, Galileo, and GLONASS individually recover ~4,200 km, the projection explanation wins and the MGEX result becomes a methodological footnote. If per-constellation analysis also yields ~1,900 km, the $L_c \leftrightarrow R_T(M_\oplus)$ identification fails as calibrated, and the Level 1/2/3 claim hierarchy (Section 8) absorbs the hit exactly as designed—Level 2 falls, Levels 1 and 3 survive. Executing the matched-day CODE/CNES comparison (Paper 14 Step 3.1), the factorial estimator decomposition (Paper 14 Step 3.5), and the 100-trial Monte Carlo ISB projection simulation in Step 14 (which proves that 4-constellation ISB removal compresses a $4{,}200$ km field to $2{,}277\text{--}2{,}485$ km, $1.62\text{--}1.77\times$, matching the empirical $1.90\times$ offset that de-biases pooled MGEX $1{,}862$ km to $3{,}536$ km) together with the executed 4-quarter 2023 CDDIS RINEX3 single-constellation reprocessing in Step 14 (where full-day $n_{\rm seg}=288$ single-constellation solutions recover $3{,}766$ km [GPS], $3{,}806$ km [GLONASS], and $3{,}653$ km [Galileo $10\text{--}60\,\mu\text{Hz}$; $2{,}697$ km full-band] with a three-constellation mean of $3{,}423$ km inside the $[3{,}000,\,5{,}000]$ km GPS-only band, and where applying MGEX's 8-hour $n_{\rm seg}=96$ window truncation, per-epoch datum removal, and 4-constellation ISB estimation compresses the exact same station-days step-by-step from $3{,}423 \to 2{,}850 \to 2{,}156$ km [$\mathrm{CV} = 0.28\%$ across GPS $2{,}163.2$, Galileo $2{,}152.9$, and GLONASS $2{,}152.3$ km] $\to 1{,}478$ km, a $2.32\times$ empirical compression) resolves the multi-GNSS compression mechanism directly on real observations.

The discriminator has now been executed (TEP-GNSS-MGEX Steps 3.1 and 3.5, TEP-GNSS-MGEX/results/outputs/step_3_1_per_center_scale.json), with the pre-registration outcome stated plainly: the literal per-constellation station-clock split could not be executed as originally designed because MGEX station clocks are published as single combined-solution parameters (one receiver clock per station-epoch combining GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, and BeiDou with inter-system bias absorption), and space-segment satellite clocks in the combined product carry no distance-decaying within-constellation coherence (Step 2.8). The substitute discriminators executed on the retained archive establish three facts: (i) on the identical COD MGEX product, quarterly windows scatter from $\lambda = 1{,}050$ to $1{,}935$ km ($R^2$ from $-0.18$ to $0.80$), showing that short $\lesssim 1$-year windows carry $\sim 30$–$40\%$ fractional sampling scatter around their product mean; (ii) on strictly matched, verified-independent days, two independent multi-GNSS processing chains — CODE/GPSEST and CNES/GINS (GRG) — return pooled $\lambda = 2{,}744$ km ($n = 43{,}971$ pairs) and $1{,}933$ km ($n = 44{,}000$) respectively ($\sim 30\%$ apart, GRG within $4\%$ of the pooled $1{,}862$ km MGEX scale); and (iii) a factorial estimator decomposition on identical station-days (Paper 14 §4.5b, Step 3.5) proves that estimator settings (Welch segment length and common-mode removal) shift $\lambda$ by $\lesssim \pm 25\%$, whereas both GPS-only IGS-final ($\lambda \approx 3.9$–$5.9\times 10^3$ km in the tidal band, isotropic $3{,}210 \pm 389$ km and cross-sector $4{,}201 \pm 1{,}967$ km over 25.3 years in Paper 2; raw-RINEX ionofree $4{,}767 \pm 835$ km in Paper 3) and 4-constellation MGEX ($\lambda \approx 1.8$–$2.0\times 10^3$ km) recover the identical band-ordered decay profile, with the MGEX combined multi-GNSS filter acting as a $\sim 2\times$ scaled-down projection of the GPS-only PPP profile. Thus, the 25.3-year temporal stability of the GPS-only CODE baseline (Paper 2) and the product-family scale offset of the 4-constellation MGEX filter (Paper 14) are distinct phenomena: a quantitative cross-mode de-biasing synthesis in Step 14 (constellation_debiasing_synthesis) comparing single-constellation GPS/ionofree RINEX and PPP solutions across identical station networks (mean $4{,}066$ km; including $4{,}767$ km raw-RINEX dual-frequency ionofree and $3{,}512$–$5{,}026$ km across ALL STATIONS and OPTIMAL 100 in Paper 3) against 4-constellation combined multi-GNSS solutions (mean $2{,}141$ km; $1{,}770$–$2{,}726$ km raw-RINEX multi-GNSS and $1{,}862$–$2{,}744$ km MGEX) establishes an empirical multi-GNSS inter-system-bias (ISB) filter compression factor of $1.90\times$. Crucially, the Monte Carlo injection–recovery suite on a 120-station global network in Step 14 proves the direction and mechanism of this bias without circular ratio rescaling: across an injected scale grid $L_c^{\rm true} \in \{1{,}000,\,2{,}000,\,3{,}000,\,4{,}200,\,6{,}000\}$ km, 1-constellation GPS-only global datum removal tracks every scale ($\hat L_c^{\rm GPS} = 1{,}019,\,1{,}975,\,2{,}912,\,4{,}036 \pm 113,\text{ and }5{,}721$ km; white-noise null returns $|C(d)| = 0.009 \pm 0.001$, and a correlated rank-3 satellite-orbit/geocenter dipole plus datum common-mode null yields a concave-down correlation curve $d^2C/dd^2 = -0.024\text{ Mm}^{-2} < 0$ whose unconstrained exponential fit hits the $\sim 24{,}900$ km upper bound rather than manufacturing a $4{,}200$ km scale), whereas 4-constellation multi-GNSS common-view ISB projection compresses $L_c^{\rm true} = 4{,}200$ km to $\hat L_c^{\rm MGEX} = 2{,}277 \pm 47$ km ($1.77 \pm 0.06\times$ compression) for per-epoch ISBs (matching CNES/GRG $1{,}933$ km) and $\hat L_c = 2{,}485 \pm 45$ km ($1.62\times$ compression) for daily-constant ISBs with rotating constellation visibility (matching CODE matched-day MGEX $2{,}744$ km). The spatial ISB projection and temporal passband truncation both suppress long-scale covariance and may describe overlapping aspects of the combined processing response; they are not treated as independent multiplicative corrections without a joint transfer calculation, with the simulated $1.62\text{--}1.77\times$ compression reproducing the direction and approximate magnitude of the observed $1.90\times$ offset (mapping pooled MGEX $1{,}862$ km to $3{,}536$ km inside the $3{,}330$–$4{,}549$ km GPS-only anchor range). Applying the $\sim 30$–$40\%$ fractional window/chain uncertainty around the GPS-only anchor ($L_c = 4{,}200$ km) under the conditional $L_c \leftrightarrow R_T(M_\oplus)$ identification yields the systematic band $\rho_T \in [8,\,70]$ g/cm³, while the unadjusted 4-constellation MGEX projection ($\lambda = 1{,}862$–$2{,}744$ km at unit prefactor) defines a conservative upper sensitivity bracket of $\rho_T \sim 69$–$220$ g/cm³. With the 4-quarter 2023 CDDIS RINEX3 single-constellation GPS, Galileo, and GLONASS reprocessing now executed in Step 14 (including Galileo dual-frequency iono-free LC recovering $L_c = 4{,}556.7\text{ km}$ across 67 optimal_100 stations), the single- versus multi-constellation discriminator is resolved on public data, leaving independent third-party geodesy replication and optical/two-way time-transfer links as the remaining external verification targets.
